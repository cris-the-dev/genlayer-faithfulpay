How it works

An escrow that reads both languages.

  1. I
    Escrow

    The client names the translator, the source page and its SHA-256, the target language, required terms and a deadline, and pays the fee. Validators fetch the source right away.

  2. II
    Deliver

    The translator publishes the translation and submits its URL and SHA-256. Both pages are pinned by hash.

  3. III
    Check

    Each required term must appear in the text. Then validators agree on the exact list of issues: omission, mistranslation, wrong language.

  4. IV
    Settle

    No issues: paid in the same transaction. Issues: listed so the translator can fix them, up to three attempts, then the client is refunded.

Built for both sides

For clients

  • Order translations into languages you can't check yourself.
  • The fee is released only for a complete, faithful translation.
  • Insist on product names, figures or legal terms with required terms.
  • Missed deadline or three failed attempts: anyone can refund you.

For translators

  • The money is locked before you start. No chasing invoices.
  • Paid the moment validators agree, not when someone gets round to it.
  • Feedback is a concrete issue list, not “we're not happy”.
  • Style and word choice are never held against you; only meaning is.
Pinned pagesThe source is bound to the hash the client gave, the translation to the hash the translator gives. If either page changes or cannot be fetched, the submission reverts and uses no attempt.
Whole pages onlyA page whose text is over 12,000 characters is refused, never cut: nothing is judged on part of the text.
What the verdict meansValidators agree only on the issue list. The one-sentence note is the leader's own and is not checked by consensus.

FAQ

Who decides whether a translation is faithful?

GenLayer validators. Each one fetches both pages itself, checks their hashes, and answers three narrow questions: is anything missing, does anything say something different, is it the right language. A result is accepted only when they agree on the same issue list.

Will I be rejected for word choice or style?

No. Only meaning counts: omissions, changed meaning and the wrong language. Phrasing a sentence differently is not an issue.

Can the translator swap the page after it is checked?

No. The translation is pinned by its SHA-256 when submitted, and the paid URL and hash stay on the job. A page that changed reverts the submission without using an attempt.

What if the translator never delivers?

After the deadline, or after three rejected attempts, anyone can trigger the refund and the fee goes back to the client. The translator can also decline, which refunds immediately.

How long are the documents?

Up to 12,000 characters of visible text per page. Longer pages are refused up front, so a client finds out before paying.
