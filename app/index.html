How a translation is judged

  1. I
    Escrow

    The client names the translator, the source page and its SHA-256, the target language, required terms and a deadline, and pays the fee. Validators fetch the source now.

  2. II
    Submit

    The translator publishes the translation and submits its URL and SHA-256.

  3. III
    Terms

    Validators fetch both pages, check both hashes, and look for each required term in the text (no AI).

  4. IV
    Faithfulness

    They agree on the exact list of issues: omission, mistranslation, wrong language.

No issuesthe fee goes to the translator; the translation's hash is stored
Issueslisted for the fix; after 3 attempts or the deadline, the client is refunded

Disclosures

Pinned pagesThe source is bound to the hash the client gave, the translation to the hash the translator gives. If either page changes or cannot be fetched, the submission reverts and uses no attempt.
Whole pages onlyA page whose text is over 12,000 characters is refused, never cut: nothing is judged on part of the text.
What the verdict meansValidators agree only on the issue list. The one-sentence note is the leader's own and is not checked by consensus.
